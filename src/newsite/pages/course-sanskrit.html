The approach

संस्कृतम्

Taught the Gurukula way: sound first, then script, then grammar, then texts.

Sanskrit opens up one sound at a time. I begin every student by listening to how they say the letters, and we build from there, slowly and properly.

Grammar is the backbone of how I teach. Without it you can memorise words, but you can’t really read. Our tradition says it plainly: without vyakarana, there is no real knowledge of Sanskrit.

Method

How I teach Sanskrit

01

Sound comes first

Every vowel and consonant is corrected live: short, long and extended sounds, aspirated and unaspirated letters, anusvara and visarga. Clean pronunciation now saves years later.

02

Devanagari by hand

You write every day. Writing the letters by hand fixes them in memory far better than typing ever does.

03

Grammar, one layer at a time

Sentences first, then noun forms (shabda rupa), then verb forms (dhatu rupa), then sandhi, samasa and karaka. Each layer rests on the one before it.

04

Reading real Sanskrit

Words become sentences, sentences become stories, and slowly you’re reading shlokas and subhashitas without needing a translation.

The path

Three levels, six stages

The full path usually takes 18 to 24 months. Everyone moves forward when they’re ready, not when the calendar says so.

Level 1 · Beginner, from scratchAbout 5 to 7 months

Sounds, script and simple sentences

Stage I (Samskrita Pravesha) and Stage II (Vakya Nirmana). You learn to say every letter correctly, write Devanagari, and start speaking simple Sanskrit.

Vowels, consonants and their correct soundsDevanagari writing practiceWords for the body, food, objects and numbersIntroducing yourself in SanskritPronouns, gender and numberQuestion words and simple sentences
Level 2 · IntermediateAbout 6 to 8 months

How words change

Stage III (Shabda Rupa) and Stage IV (Dhatu Rupa). You see how nouns and verbs change their form, and why.

Noun forms: Rama, Rama (feminine), Phala, Hari, Guru, PitrPronouns: asmad, yushmad, tad, kimThe seven vibhaktis (cases)Common dhatus and their formsPresent, past, future and command (lat, lan, lrt, lot)
Level 3 · AdvancedAbout 7 to 9 months

Traditional grammar

Stage V (Vyakarana Pravesha) and Stage VI (Shastra Samskritam). Formal grammar, and then the Laghu Siddhanta Kaumudi, so you can open a text and read it yourself.

Sandhi: vowel, consonant and visargaSamasa: tatpurusha, karmadharaya, dvandva, bahuvrihiKaraka: how a sentence really worksLaghu Siddhanta Kaumudi, selected prakaranasReading shlokas, subhashitas and small granthas
